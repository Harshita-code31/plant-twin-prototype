# AbhyasAR — Presenter-led Showcase Video

**Format:** one person speaks to camera the whole way through. The app screens are laid over their voice at set moments, then the video comes back to the presenter.
**Length:** about 2 min 45 s (a 45-second cut is at the end)
**Look:** 16:9, 1080p. Presenter standing next to a wall-mounted fire extinguisher, so the set itself says "safety".
**Tone:** friendly and practical. Talk to a safety manager or plant supervisor, like you're showing a colleague something useful.
**Saying the name:** "AB-yaas A-R" (*abhyas* means "practice" in Hindi, which is the whole idea of the app).

**How it fits together**
1. **Part A** is the clean text for the presenter to read (teleprompter). No cues, just words.
2. **Part B** is the cue sheet for the editor: what appears over the presenter's voice, when, and how.
3. **Part C** is the shooting and editing guide, plus a list of screen clips to record.

Overlay modes used in Part B:
- **ON CAMERA** – presenter fills the frame.
- **FULL + VO** – app screen fills the frame, presenter is heard only (voiceover).
- **PiP** – app screen fills the frame, presenter in a small window in a corner (bottom-right) so they stay present.
- **SPLIT** – presenter on the left third, phone screen on the right two thirds.
- **LOWER THIRD** – text bar over the presenter.

## PART A — What the presenter says (teleprompter text)

> Read at a relaxed pace, about 150 words a minute. Pauses are marked with a line break. Around 400 words.

**[1 · Hook]**
Most safety training gets read, signed… and forgotten.

And then, when something actually goes wrong, people freeze.

**[2 · What it is]**
This is AbhyasAR. It lets your workers *practise* safety, hands-on, in augmented reality, on their own phone.

Every worker has their own login. It works offline after the first sign-in, and the whole app speaks English, Hindi and Santali.

**[3 · Fire Safety]**
Let me show you. I open Fire Safety, point the phone at the floor, and tap once.

A fire safety station appears in the room I'm standing in. A call point on the wall, and three different extinguishers.

Now a fire starts. And notice, I can't just grab an extinguisher. I have to raise the alarm first, the way I would for real.

Then I have to choose the *right* extinguisher for this fire. Pick the wrong one, and the app stops me.

**[4 · PASS]**
Then it's the technique everyone should know: P-A-S-S.

Pull the pin. Aim at the base of the fire. Squeeze the lever. Sweep side to side.

And the app only gives points for what you actually did. Skip a step, and you don't get credit for it.

**[5 · Scenarios]**
There are four different fires: a bin, a power outlet, an electrical cabinet, and a gas furnace. On the furnace, if you forget to shut off the gas… it explodes. People remember that.

**[6 · Machine Training]**
It's not only fire. Machine Training puts a conveyor belt in front of you, and you practise the controls: start, stop, the emergency stop, reset, manual jog, and speed.

**[7 · Quiz and score]**
After the practice comes a ten-question quiz. Practice counts for forty percent, the quiz for sixty, and you need eighty to pass.

**[8 · Certificate]**
Pass, and a certificate appears straight away, with a QR code. You can zoom in, save it to your gallery, and share it.

Scan that code, and anyone can check it's real.

**[9 · Dashboard]**
For supervisors, there's a dashboard. Create logins for a whole team in one go. See who has trained, who has passed, and which fire steps people miss most. Export it all to a spreadsheet.

**[10 · Offline]**
And if there's no signal on the shop floor? No problem. Everything is saved on the phone and syncs by itself when it's back online.

**[11 · Close]**
AbhyasAR. Safety you've actually practised.

## PART C — Shooting and editing guide

### The presenter shoot
- **Frame:** medium shot (waist up), presenter slightly off centre, a wall-mounted extinguisher in the background. Leave the bottom-right quarter clear, because that's where the PiP window goes.
- **Camera:** eye level, 1080p or 4K, 25 or 30 fps. Use the same frame rate for the screen recordings.
- **Sound matters most:** lapel or shotgun mic close to the mouth, quiet room. Record a 20-second silence for noise removal.
- **Delivery:** read Part A from a teleprompter or cue cards placed next to the lens. Do the whole thing in 2 or 3 full takes, then pick the best lines. Pause a beat between numbered sections so they're easy to cut.
- **Hand actions:** a few natural gestures help the overlays land: tapping a phone on "tap once", holding up three fingers on "three different extinguishers", counting P-A-S-S on the fingers.
- **Wardrobe:** plain colour that isn't red, grey or yellow, since those are the extinguisher colours in the app.
- **Clap or tap** on the mic at the very start of each take so the editor can sync.

### The screen clips (record these separately)
On a real ARCore Android phone with the latest build (`Builds/Android/AbhyasAR.apk`). AR doesn't work in the Unity editor.

1. Login → main menu → language switching (English, Hindi, Santali)
2. Fire Safety: floor scan → tap → station appears
3. Alarm blocked → alarm raised → wrong extinguisher refused → right extinguisher
4. Pin pull (close-up), aim, squeeze with smoke, sweep, fire out
5. Training Result panel with one ✗ step
6. Each of the four fire scenarios (10 seconds each), including the furnace explosion
7. Machine Training: each control, checklist ticking
8. Quiz question with an image, result popup, results panel
9. Certificate opening, pinch-zoom, Save to Gallery
10. Second phone scanning the QR → "Certificate verified" page
11. Dashboard: Overview, Workers, Add workers, worker detail, CSV export (browser at 1920×1080, 100% zoom)
12. Airplane-mode drill, then the sync catching up

**Recording tips**
- Android's built-in screen recorder, or `adb shell screenrecord --size 1080x2400 /sdcard/x.mp4` then `adb pull /sdcard/x.mp4`.
- Use a **demo worker** from the dashboard, with both trainings passed so the certificate list shows two. Never use a real employee's name or ID.
- Bright, uncluttered floor and wall. Blank glossy floors are slow for plane detection.
- Do Not Disturb on, notifications off, battery above 50%, brightness up.
- Record each clip a little longer than needed (2 s of handles at the start and end).

### Editing the overlays
- **Timeline layers:** presenter video on the bottom; screen clips above it; text and lower thirds on top; music under everything.
- **Phone clips are tall (9:16).** In FULL mode, place the phone centred on a blurred, darkened copy of the presenter frame, or on a plain dark background, with a thin rounded frame. Don't stretch it.
- **PiP:** presenter window about 25% of the frame, rounded corners, bottom-right. Keep the presenter's audio on the whole time.
- **Speed:** don't cut away from a screen recording mid-action. Speed up the boring parts (scanning, typing) 2–4× and slow the key moments (pin pull, smoke) to 50%.
- **Text:** one short phrase at a time, large, bottom third, same font as the app's headings.
- **Captions:** burn in English captions for silent autoplay, and export a separate `.srt`.
- **Music:** calm, steady, low; duck about 12 dB under speech. No music on the very first line.
- **Safe zones:** if you also export vertical (9:16), keep the presenter's face in the centre third.

### Claims to avoid
- The certificate is a record of completing this training in the app, not a legal or regulatory credential.
- Don't say it replaces hands-on practice with a real extinguisher, or that it meets any specific fire-code requirement.
- Spoken narration exists for English and Hindi. Santali screens use the Hindi voice.
- It is a training simulation, not real fire behaviour.
- Don't show real worker IDs or passwords. Dashboard passwords show only once when created, so use dummy ones.
